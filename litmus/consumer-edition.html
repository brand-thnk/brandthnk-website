What this is

A bank CMO fed her campaign doc, pre-research, and personas into an AI assistant and got directionally useful panel feedback in an afternoon. Litmus Consumer Edition turns that instinct into a repeatable system.

How Litmus works

1

Ground

Start from real research: your pre-research and focus groups, market assessments, local demographic data.

2

Build

Construct personas on the 8-layer behavioral model. Every trait traces to a source.

3

Convene

Seat 3–5 personas spanning the campaign's actual audience segments. They react in first person, one message at a time.

4

Pressure-test

Every element — tagline, posts, scripts, web copy. What resonated, what read as bank-speak, what they'd say in their own words.

5

Translate

Plain-language audit against CFPB standards. A keep/change board. Channel weighting checked against media research.

The model behind it

Litmus was built on an 8-layer behavioral model for B2B buyers — C-suite decision-makers at banks and credit unions. Consumer Edition keeps the architecture and re-aims every layer at the person on the other side of the campaign.

LayerConsumer Edition
1Identity — life stage, work, household
2Money context — income rhythm, financial situation
3Banking relationship — current accounts, history, who holds their money now
4Channel & media life — where they actually pay attention
5Psychographics — money mindset, decision style, trust signals
6Pain points — fees, friction, past bad experiences
7Decision context — the live life moment, what would make them move
8Messaging receptivity — language that works and fails

Guardrails

CE-01 · Seated Panelist

Jalen, 18

Recent high school graduate | First full-time job | New to banking | Price Hill, Cincinnati
Layers 1–2: Identity & Money Context
Life stage

Just graduated, just started his first full-time job. Banking decisions are his to make for the first time.

Money context

First steady paycheck, no cushion yet. Fee-sensitive and speed-sensitive: the two questions are "does my paycheck hit fast" and "do I get fee'd."

Layers 3–4: Banking & Channels
Banking relationship

New to banking independently. His money lives on his phone — Cash App and a banking app. "Money as a place" is not a concept he uses.

Channel & media life

Instagram and TikTok — "not Facebook, that's for my mom." Radio only in someone else's car. Notices transit ads on the bus but won't act on them; needs to see it online where he can click and do something.

Layers 5–7: Psychology & Decision Context
Money mindset

"I want my money to actually do something for me — not just sit there getting eaten by fees." Community reinvestment is a nice-to-have after his own needs are met — his side first.

Decision style & trust

Decides fast. Skips opening paragraphs, goes straight to CTA buttons. Trust comes from a friend's vouch and an app that looks legit — not a Community Impact page.

Pain points

Fees. Slow paycheck access. Brands trying to sound young and missing — copy that feels "written by someone older trying to sound relatable."

Live trigger

Choosing his first real bank right now, because the first job made it real.

Layer 8: Messaging Receptivity
✓ Language that resonates
  • "Your paycheck hits fast and you don't get fee'd" — the concrete functional promise, first.
  • Screenshot-able lines: "Big financial milestones grow one deposit at a time."
  • Direct CTAs: Open an Account. That's what he came for.
✕ Language that repels
  • "Where your money lives" — money-as-place metaphors read as older-person speak.
  • 60 seconds of ambient soundscape before you say who's talking.
  • Teaser lines: "Something better is coming" — "coming from where?"

Grounded in: Warsaw Federal Consumer Research Project Report (Kendrick, May 2025) — focus groups with banked, under-banked, and unbanked West Side residents · Price Hill demographic profile · Nielsen Diverse Intelligence audio research.

Maria, 36

Working mother | Two years from a first home | Existing bank relationship | Price Hill, Cincinnati
Layers 1–2: Identity & Money Context
Life stage

Working mother running a daily school drop-off and pickup routine. The household's financial planner.

Money context

Budget-conscious, saving toward a down payment on a two-year homeownership horizon.

Banking relationship

Has a bank. Would move for a lender she believes won't jerk her around on a mortgage.

Channel & media life

Radio every morning in the car — drop-off and pickup. Social works when it shows real families, not stock photos. Billboards build "I've heard of them," not decisions.

Layers 5–7: Psychology & Decision Context
Money mindset

Thinks in family stability and neighborhood terms: "my mortgage payment isn't just for me, it's helping the neighborhood too." Wants a bank that cares "if my street looks better in five years."

Decision style & trust

Reads carefully but rewards directness — "I don't need a metaphor about a tricycle. Just talk to me directly." Explained concepts earn her; stacked jargon loses her.

Pain points

Fear of getting jerked around on a mortgage. Three unfamiliar concepts stacked in one sentence ("customer-owned mutual Minority Depository Institution") — she had to reread it.

Live trigger

House-hunting within two years. Closing-cost assistance is a real draw, not a footnote.

Layer 8: Messaging Receptivity
✓ Language that resonates
  • "A Home for You. A Stronger Neighborhood for Everyone." — "I pictured moving boxes into my own place. Keep exactly as is."
  • "Some banks move money. Warsaw Federal helps communities move forward." — a distinction she gets fast, while driving.
  • MDI explained in one breath — intrigued once the mission is unpacked.
✕ Language that repels
  • "Customer-owned mutual" with no translation — two unfamiliar concepts in one phrase.
  • Radio spots that take too long to say who's talking to her.
  • Precious metaphors standing in for direct talk.

✓ Language that resonates

Results · Warsaw Federal

Takeaways

The campaign's underlying logic — personal financial benefit connected to community impact — is validated. All three panelists described that exact logic in their own words, unprompted, before seeing the campaign language. The risk isn't the strategy. It's execution: phrases built on financial-industry abstractions go over the audience's head, while homeownership and business messaging over-performs because it maps to concrete, felt life moments.

Details

Panelists were asked to describe — forgetting the campaign — what it means to use their money well and help their community. None used "mutual," "reinvest," "financial solutions," or "relationship-based." All three described the strategy anyway.

"I want my money to actually do something for me — not just sit there getting eaten by fees. And if the bank I use is also, like, from around here and reinvests in stuff I can see — that's cool, but honestly I care more about my side of it first."

Recommendations

Keep

  • "Better for You. Better for Your Community." — tested strongest across all three panelists once paired with concrete outcomes.
  • Homeownership & business messaging — highest resonance across social, radio, and web. Concrete, felt, specific.
  • The 30-second brand spot — "Some banks move money…" Tightest, clearest, most memorable of the three scripts.
  • CTA-driven homepage — Open an Account / Explore Business Banking. Direct, functional, zero complaints.
  • Billboard & transit as a legitimacy layer — especially given Price Hill's transit-dependent population.

Change

  • "Where Your Money Lives Matters" — weakest phrase in the campaign, flagged in every review pass. Simplify, or reassign to an education page.
  • "Something better is coming" — reads as a teaser for a future launch, not a live campaign. Needs a present-tense close.
  • 60-second spot soundscape — generic "diverse neighborhood" shorthand instead of the specific texture of Black and Latino Price Hill. Co-develop with community voices.
  • Homepage opener — MDI + mutual + customer-owned stacked in one sentence. Lead with MDI; let "mutual" earn its explanation.
  • Radio's slot in the sequence — under-weighted. Move it into the first wave.
  • Bilingual strategy — currently absent from a plan that explicitly targets Latino community members. Needs a decision, even if phased.

Bank-speak audit

Campaign copy tested against CFPB plain-writing standards and against how the panel talked, unprompted. The pattern: the audience already believes the idea — the words keep getting in the way.

As writtenThe problemPlain-language alternative
"Where Your Money Lives Matters"Assumes the reader understands deposit-to-lending flow; money-as-place is not how people describe their accounts"Bank here, and your money helps your neighbors too."
"Customer-owned mutual bank"Two unfamiliar concepts stacked in one phrase"A bank owned by the people who bank here — not stockholders."
"Minority Depository Institution (MDI)"Technical designation — but research shows real intrigue once explainedKeep the term; always pair it with a one-sentence plain explanation.
"Expanding access to financial opportunity"Grant-language; a mission statement, not a conversation"Making it easier for more people to get a loan, open an account, and get ahead."
"Relationship-based banking"Internal industry term, not customer language"Bankers who actually know your business, not just your file."
"Reinvests locally"Passive, institutional-finance verb"Keeps money working right here in the neighborhood."

The framework is yours.

One CMO in this network already proved the instinct works with the tools she had. Below is the framework and what it takes to make the output research-grade, repeatable, and yours.

Starter prompt

Runs in whatever assistant your IT allows, fill the brackets, and attach your research.

CONTEXT You are helping [BANK] test campaign messaging before production. Attached: the campaign messaging document, our pre-research readout, and our market assessment / personas. The bank is [one-line identity — e.g., "an MDI and community mutual bank serving X"]. Campaign goal: [goal]. ROLE Act as the moderator of a simulated consumer research panel. Seat [3–5] personas drawn from the campaign's actual audience segments: [list them]. Build each persona ONLY from the attached research and cited local data. If the research doesn't support a trait, leave it out. INTERVIEW Before the panel starts, ask me up to 3 questions, one at a time, to close context gaps. TASK Walk the panel through every message element — tagline, pillars, each social post, each script, web copy. For each: first-person reactions, what resonated, what read as jargon, and what they'd say in their own words. Then run a plain-language audit against CFPB plain-writing standards. RULES — Directional, not definitive: this sharpens copy ahead of formal testing. It does not replace it. — Label every quote as simulated. — Cite the source behind every persona trait and every external claim. — The panel's job is to find weak language, not to validate the campaign. OUTPUT A readout with: executive summary, top 3 takeaways, per-section reactions with verbatims, a keep/change board, a bank-speak audit table with plain-language alternatives, channel notes, and a full source list.
